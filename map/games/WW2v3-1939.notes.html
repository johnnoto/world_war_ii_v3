World War II v3 – September 1939

Scenario by johnnoto · Version 2026-10-29 · Both players in an online game should see the same version here.

1 September 1939. The Wehrmacht crosses into Poland. Two days later Britain and France declare war. The Soviet Union has just signed a non-aggression pact with Germany, Italy is a "non-belligerent" bound to Berlin by the Pact of Steel, Japan has been at war in China for two years, and the United States is officially neutral. Nobody yet knows how wide this war will become – that is up to you.

1. About This Scenario

This scenario starts the World War II v3 map at the outbreak of war, two years before the 1941 game. It adds:

  • France as a full eighth power, with its own army, fleet, colonies and violet units.
  • Neutral countries with their own armies (orange units) that anyone may invade.
  • Diplomacy: powers start at peace and go to war through declarations in the Politics phase, each available only when history allows.
  • Historical events that fire on their own: Italy's entry, the Fall of France and Vichy, the Balkans and Finland joining the Axis, the Dutch East Indies, America's entry and more.
  • Briefings: at the start of its first turn, each power receives a letter from its high command describing its situation in September 1939, with a short timeline of what lies ahead.

Each power's national anthem plays at the start of its turn, for every player. It can be muted with Triggered Notification Sound in the sound options.

The game is played until one side surrenders; victory cities can be switched on under Game Options on the setup screen (section 10).

Events announce themselves with pop-up messages. If you don't see them, open Game > User Notifications and tick Show Triggered Notifications. The Objectives tab next to the map shows every national objective and a Diplomacy and Events list, each marked when it is currently in effect.

7. Events in Detail

7.1 The Fall of France

  • French factory destroyed. The first time France passes to Germany – taken by Germany, or by Italy and handed over – the French factory is removed at the end of that combat phase.
  • The armistice. If the Axis still hold France when Germany's next Politics phase begins – so Britain and France have had a full round to retake Paris – the Armistice of Compiègne is signed:
    • Morocco-Algeria goes over to Germany, always with a garrison of 2 German infantry, whatever French troops were there.
    • French West Africa, French Equatorial Africa and Madagascar (and the Belgian Congo, if it had joined France), with their garrisons, go over to Britain.
    • French land and air units anywhere else on land – moved out of the empire, or fighting alongside the British – join Britain where they stand, unit for unit (up to 6 infantry, 3 artillery, 3 armour, 3 fighters, 1 bomber and 1 AA gun in each territory). French troops still in Morocco-Algeria do not: Germany always receives exactly 2 infantry there.
    • French Indo-China stays with Vichy France but loses its garrison. Vichy France and Japan are at war, so Japan needs no declaration (there is no button for it): it may simply move troops in during any combat move, with no wider consequences.
    • The French navy is removed. If France still has any warship, Britain receives one cruiser in sea zone 2, off western Britain, or off Halifax (sea zone 9) if the Axis hold the United Kingdom.
    • The remaining French units (in Morocco-Algeria and Indo-China, and at sea, including aircraft on carriers) are disbanded, and France is at peace with everyone except Japan for the rest of the game.
  • No liberation. After the armistice, whoever takes France keeps it. France does not return.
  • Germany reinforced. If Germany attacks France and, on France's next turn, France still holds Paris, is still at war and builds new units in France, Germany receives 5 infantry in Germany at the end of France's turn (once per game). Holding Paris without building there is not enough.

7.2 Governments in Exile

  • Belgian Congo. The moment the Axis take Northwestern Europe – at the end of that combat phase – the Belgian Congo joins Britain – or France, if the Axis already hold the United Kingdom while France is still at war and holds Paris. A Congo that joined France goes over to Britain if France later signs the armistice.
  • Dutch East Indies. Borneo and the East Indies stay neutral and closed until Japan and Britain are at war. They join Britain, with 1 infantry each, at the end of the Politics phase in which that war begins: if Japan declares, Japan can attack them that same turn; if Britain declares, Britain holds them at once and collects their income at the end of its turn.

7.3 If London Falls

  • Whoever takes the United Kingdom captures the PUs Britain had in hand, as for any captured capital.
  • The USA and the USSR declare war on Germany at the end of that combat phase.
  • The government moves to Ottawa. Eastern Canada becomes a second British capital and receives a factory, so Britain keeps collecting income, building and fighting. This needs Britain to hold Eastern Canada; otherwise it happens as soon as Britain does.
  • Japan may declare war on Britain at once, even in round 1.
  • The Empire fights on. If Ottawa falls too, the government moves again: to Australia, then South Africa, then India, then New Zealand. Each move happens at the end of the combat phase in which Britain loses its last seat of government, if it still holds the next one; the new seat becomes a British capital, with a factory if it has none. Each time, the PUs Britain had in hand go to whoever took the fallen capital.
  • The fall of the Empire. If London, Ottawa, Australia, South Africa, India and New Zealand are all lost, Britain is out of the war: its remaining units are disbanded and any territory it still holds passes to the United States.

7.4 Germany's Allies and the East

  • Italy sits out round 1 and enters the war automatically on its round-2 turn.
  • German sphere. If Italy captures France, Northwestern Europe, Poland, Bulgaria-Romania, Norway or Finland, the territory goes to Germany instead. Italian units there stay Italian.
  • Bulgaria-Romania and Finland join Germany in round 3 (section 4).
  • Operation Barbarossa is possible from round 3. Italy follows Germany into the war.
  • The Soviets strike first. If Germany has not attacked by the end of the USSR's round-5 turn, the USSR declares war on Germany.

7.5 Asia and the Pacific

  • Crisis in Indo-China. If Japan declares war on France while France is still at war with Germany: Britain may declare war on Japan; the USA enters the war a round early (end of its round-3 turn); and the USA places 1 infantry and 1 AA gun each in Hawaii and the Philippines.
  • India falls. If Japan takes India, the USA declares war on Japan at once.
  • The southern advance. If Japan holds Borneo, the East Indies and French Indo-China at the same time, the USA declares war on Japan at once.
  • China's supply routes. See section 9.
  • Britain and Japan. Britain may declare war on Japan from round 3 (in its Politics phase, just before its combat move). The Dutch East Indies then join Britain with their garrisons.
  • August Storm. The USSR may declare war on Japan on any of its turns, from round 1. Japan may strike first from round 2 (Strike North).

7.6 When America Enters

The USA is at war with Germany, Italy and Japan by the end of its round-4 turn at the latest. It goes to war sooner if:

  • Japan attacks Indo-China while France is still fighting (end of round 3);
  • the Axis take London (war with Germany, then Italy follows);
  • Japan takes India, or holds Borneo, the East Indies and Indo-China together (war with Japan);
  • Japan declares war on the USA, or on Britain (the USA may then declare war on Japan).

Once Japan is at war with both Britain and the USA, Germany declares war on the USA and Italy follows.

7.7 The World Divided

If the Axis hold Washington (Eastern United States), London and Moscow at the same time, the victors turn on each other: Japan may declare war on Germany and on Italy, and Germany and Italy may declare war on Japan, each in its own Politics phase.

9. China

  • China has no PUs and no factory. Each turn it receives 1 infantry for every 2 territories it controls.
  • Chinese units may be placed in any territory China controls, including captured ones, up to 3 per territory.
  • Chinese units may only move within China: Chinghai, Ningxia, Sikang, Yunnan, Hupeh, Fukien, Suiyuan, Manchuria, Kiangsu and Kwangtung.
  • Supply routes. At the start of each Chinese Place Units phase, as long as China holds Yunnan, it receives in Yunnan: 1 artillery if France, Britain, the USA or China holds French Indo-China (the Hanoi–Kunming railway), and 1 more if any of them holds Burma (the Burma Road). Vichy France counts as France.

10. Victory

By surrender (the default)

The game does not end on its own. It is played until one side concedes: when the Axis or the Allies can no longer win, their players surrender and the other side wins. This lets a war be fought to its real conclusion, and lets either side fight on from Ottawa, the Urals or the last island if it believes it can still turn things around.

By victory cities (optional)

To end the game automatically, press Game Options on the setup screen before starting and tick a victory condition: Projection of Power (the shortest game), Honorable Surrender or Total Victory. A side wins if it holds the required number of the 18 victory cities at the end of a round. Each target can be changed in the same window; Make Default keeps your choice for future games.

SideHolds at the start
Axis4: Germany, Italy, Japan, Kiangsu (Shanghai)
Allies13: United Kingdom, Eastern Canada, India, Australia, Kwangtung, Eastern and Western United States, Hawaii, Philippines, Russia, Karelia, Caucasus, France

Poland (Warsaw), the eighteenth victory city, starts neutral. The Allies count the neutral USA and USSR from the start.

Recommended victory-city settings

ConditionAxisAlliesWhat it means
Projection of Power (recommended)1315A decisive win for either side.
Honorable Surrender1517A longer game that goes most of the way to the end.
Total Victory1818Every victory city: in practice the same as playing to surrender.

These are the default targets when you tick a condition. Why 13 for the Axis and 15 for the Allies:

  • The Axis usually reach 6 cities early with Warsaw and Paris. Getting to 13 means seven more: Hong Kong, Manila, Calcutta, Leningrad, Moscow and Stalingrad, plus one of London, Ottawa, Sydney or Honolulu. That is the Soviet Union and India broken – a war the Axis have won.
  • The Allies usually fall to around 10 to 12 cities once Paris, Hong Kong and Manila are lost. Getting back to 15 means retaking that ground and pushing into the Axis homelands – liberating Paris and Warsaw and taking Rome or Shanghai. That is a war the Allies have won. The old target of 17 forced the Allies to take four of the five Axis cities while losing nothing, which in practice only came long after the war was decided.
  • The two targets ask each side for a similar share of the enemy's cities, so neither side wins on a technicality.

11. Game Settings

  • World War II v3 rules, units and technology, as in the 1941 and 1942 scenarios. National objectives, technology development, politics and triggers are on. Low Luck is off.
  • Neutral countries cost nothing to invade; aircraft may not fly over them.
  • Units that appear through events (for example the Romanian and Bulgarian armies, or the garrisons of the Dutch East Indies) can move and attack in the combat move of the turn they appear.
  • Battleships take two hits; subs may submerge and sneak attack; shipyards are used; heavy bombers roll two dice.
